Adventure

Adventure games focus on exploration, storytelling, discovery and progression. Players are often placed into detailed worlds where they uncover locations, solve problems, interact with characters and progress through a narrative. Unlike genres that focus mainly on competition or combat, adventure games often make the journey itself the main experience. The player progresses by exploring environments, discovering information and completing objectives.

History //

Adventure games have existed since the early years of video games. Early adventure titles were heavily based around text, puzzles and player decisions because computers had limited graphical capabilities.

As technology improved, adventure games became increasingly visual. Developers were able to create larger environments, animated characters and more detailed stories for players to experience.

The development of 3D graphics expanded the genre even further. Players could now physically explore three- dimensional environments rather than moving between static screens.

Modern adventure games frequently combine exploration with action, puzzles, cinematic storytelling and player choice. This has allowed the genre to overlap with open-world, action and narrative-driven games.
