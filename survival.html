Survival

Survival games challenge players to remain alive while managing limited resources, dangerous environments and persistent threats. Instead of simply reaching the end of a level, survival games often require players to continuously search for food, equipment, materials or shelter while adapting to the world around them.

Core Idea //

Survival games create tension by making resources valuable. Items that might be unlimited in other genres can become essential to staying alive.

Players are often responsible for deciding when to explore, when to fight and when avoiding danger is the better option.

Common Mechanics //

Resource gathering, crafting, health management, exploration, inventory management and environmental hazards are common features of the genre.

Some survival games also include hunger, thirst, temperature, shelter construction or permanent consequences for death.

History //

Survival mechanics have existed in games for decades. Earlier games created survival pressure through limited health, ammunition, lives and other resources.

Survival horror games later made resource scarcity a major part of their design. Limited ammunition and healing items encouraged players to avoid unnecessary confrontations and carefully manage what they carried.

During the 2000s and 2010s, the genre expanded beyond horror. Crafting systems, large environments and procedural generation allowed survival itself to become the central objective of an entire game.

Modern survival games can take place in forests, oceans, frozen landscapes, ruined cities and even alien worlds. Despite these different settings, they share the same central question: how long can the player survive with the resources available to them?

Evolution of Survival //

As game worlds became larger and more interactive, survival systems became increasingly complex.

Players could gather materials from the environment, create tools, build shelters and gradually transform dangerous spaces into areas they could control.

This helped move survival from a mechanic used by other genres into a recognisable genre of its own.

Representative Games //

These games represent different stages in the development of survival game design.

1996

Resident Evil

Helped popularise survival horror through limited resources, exploration, inventory management and dangerous enemies.

2013

Don't Starve

Places survival itself at the centre of the game, requiring players to gather resources, craft equipment and prepare for increasingly dangerous conditions.

2018

Subnautica

Combines survival mechanics with underwater exploration, crafting, base construction and environmental storytelling on an alien planet.

Why Survival Matters //

Survival games change the value of ordinary actions. Finding an item, reaching shelter or discovering a source of resources can become meaningful because the player's continued survival depends upon them.

The genre also gives players responsibility for their own preparation. Success often depends less on completing a fixed sequence of objectives and more on understanding the environment and making good decisions.
