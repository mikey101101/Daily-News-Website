// ARCHIVE: PEOPLE / GAMING HISTORY

Influential Figures

The pioneers who transformed electronic experiments into an industry that changed entertainment forever.

Before the Industry //

Video games began as experiments in science and computing. Engineers and programmers gradually developed new ways for people to interact with machines, laying the foundations for arcade games and home consoles.

Historical Timeline //

1952

A. S. Douglas

Early Computer Games

Created OXO, a computer version of noughts and crosses, as part of his doctoral research at the University of Cambridge.

IMPACT // Demonstrated how computers could be used for interactive games.

1958

William Higinbotham

Interactive Entertainment

Developed Tennis for Two, an electronic tennis game displayed on an oscilloscope at Brookhaven National Laboratory.

IMPACT // Showed how interactive electronic displays could entertain public audiences.

1962

Steve Russell

Computer Game Programming

Led the creation of Spacewar! with fellow programmers at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology.

IMPACT // Helped inspire later computer and arcade game developers.

1966–1972

Ralph Baer

Home Console Pioneer

Led the development of television game prototypes that became the Magnavox Odyssey, released in 1972.

IMPACT // Helped establish the home video game console market.

1972

Nolan Bushnell

Arcade Gaming Industry

Co-founded Atari with Ted Dabney and helped bring Pong to arcade audiences, making coin-operated video games commercially popular.

IMPACT // Helped turn video games into a commercial entertainment industry.
