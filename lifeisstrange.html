GAME ARCHIVE // ADVENTURE // 2015

Life is Strange

Life is Strange is an episodic adventure game developed by Dontnod Entertainment and released in 2015. The game follows photography student Max Caulfield after she discovers that she can rewind time. The player explores environments, interacts with characters and makes decisions that can influence later events in the story.

Game Data //

Released: 2015

Developer: Dontnod Entertainment

Publisher: Square Enix

Original Platforms: PC, PlayStation 3, PlayStation 4, Xbox 360 and Xbox One

Genre: Graphic Adventure

Archive // 003

Life is Strange represents a different direction for adventure games, placing greater emphasis on narrative, relationships and player decisions.

Instead of making combat the main challenge, progression is driven primarily through exploration, conversation and choices.

Overview //

Players control Max while exploring locations around the fictional town of Arcadia Bay. Objects and environmental details can be examined while characters can be approached and spoken to.

Conversations frequently require the player to make choices. Some decisions affect relationships immediately, while others influence events that occur later in the story.

History //

By the 2010s, narrative-focused adventure games had experienced renewed popularity. Developers increasingly experimented with games where player choice and storytelling were more important than traditional action mechanics.

Life is Strange was released episodically across five episodes. This structure allowed the story and consequences of previous decisions to develop over time.

Its focus on ordinary environments, personal relationships and difficult decisions demonstrated how the adventure genre could support smaller-scale and character-driven stories.

Choice & Interaction //

Max's ability to rewind time is both a story element and a gameplay mechanic. Players can reverse recent actions and explore alternative responses during conversations or events.

However, the game does not allow every consequence to be permanently avoided. The player is regularly required to decide which outcome they are willing to accept.

This makes decision-making a central part of the adventure rather than simply a dialogue system added around the gameplay.

Why It Matters //

Life is Strange demonstrates how adventure games can create engagement through characters, atmosphere and decisions rather than relying primarily on combat.

It also shows the continued evolution of the genre from puzzle-focused early adventures toward experiences where narrative choice can become one of the player's main forms of interaction.
