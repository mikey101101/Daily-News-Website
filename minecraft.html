// OVERVIEW

Minecraft is a sandbox video game centred around exploration, construction, survival and player creativity. The game places the player inside a procedurally generated world made primarily from blocks. These blocks represent materials such as stone, wood, dirt and ores that can be collected and used by the player.

Unlike games that require players to follow a fixed sequence of levels, Minecraft gives players considerable freedom over what they choose to do. Players can explore the world, construct buildings, collect resources, fight hostile creatures or experiment with the game's systems.

// HISTORY

Development of Minecraft began in 2009 when Swedish programmer Markus Persson began developing an experimental block-based building game. Early versions were made available to players while development was still taking place.

Minecraft gradually expanded with additional environments, creatures, resources and gameplay mechanics. The full version of the game was officially released in 2011, by which point Minecraft had already developed a large international player community.

// GAMEPLAY

Minecraft is built around interacting with and modifying the environment. Players can break blocks, collect resources and place those blocks elsewhere in the world. These simple mechanics allow players to create structures ranging from small shelters to enormous cities and complex machines.

ARCHIVE NOTE // HISTORICAL IMPACT

// HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE

Minecraft became an important example of how sandbox game design could give players greater control over their own experiences. Rather than relying entirely on predetermined levels and objectives, Minecraft provides interconnected systems that players can use creatively.

Its popularity also demonstrated the potential for independently developed games to reach extremely large audiences. Minecraft's emphasis on creativity, exploration and user-generated experiences has influenced many games that followed it.
