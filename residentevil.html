GAME ARCHIVE // SURVIVAL // 1996

Resident Evil

Resident Evil is a survival horror game developed and published by Capcom. Released in 1996 for the original PlayStation, it follows members of the S.T.A.R.S. team as they investigate a mysterious mansion outside Raccoon City.

Game Data //

Released: 1996

Developer: Capcom

Publisher: Capcom

Original Platform: PlayStation

Genre: Survival Horror

Archive // 001

Resident Evil helped establish survival horror as a recognisable genre.

Instead of encouraging the player to fight every enemy, limited ammunition, healing items and inventory space make avoiding danger an important strategy.

Overview //

Players explore the Spencer Mansion while solving puzzles, discovering keys and searching for resources.

The mansion gradually becomes more accessible as new items are discovered. Previously locked rooms and pathways can be revisited, making exploration an important part of progression.

Survival Mechanics //

Ammunition and healing items are limited, meaning the player cannot always rely on combat. Deciding whether an enemy is worth fighting becomes part of the challenge.

Inventory space is also restricted. Players must decide which weapons, keys, healing items and puzzle objects are important enough to carry.

These limitations create tension because poor preparation can make later areas considerably more difficult.

History //

Horror had appeared in games before Resident Evil, but the game helped popularise a particular combination of horror, exploration, puzzles and resource management.

Its mansion design created an interconnected environment where players repeatedly returned to familiar locations as they discovered new ways to progress.

Resident Evil became an important influence on later horror games and helped establish survival horror as a major videogame genre.

Why It Matters //

Resident Evil demonstrated that limiting the player's resources could make ordinary gameplay decisions more meaningful.

Ammunition, health and inventory space became sources of tension. This helped distinguish survival horror from action games where enemies are primarily obstacles intended to be defeated.
