GAME ARCHIVE // OPEN WORLD // 2017

The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild

The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild is an open-world action-adventure game developed and published by Nintendo. Released in 2017, the game places Link within a vast version of Hyrule and gives the player considerable freedom over where to explore and how to approach challenges.

Game Data //

Released: 2017

Developer: Nintendo EPD

Publisher: Nintendo

Original Platforms: Nintendo Switch / Wii U

Genre: Open World / Action-Adventure

Archive // 003

Breath of the Wild represents a player-driven approach to modern open-world design.

Rather than constantly directing the player toward objectives, the game encourages curiosity and allows landmarks in the environment to guide exploration.

Overview //

After leaving the opening area, players can travel through Hyrule with relatively few restrictions.

Mountains can be climbed, rivers crossed and distant locations reached using a combination of movement, equipment and the game's physical systems.

This creates a world where the player frequently decides both the destination and the route used to reach it.

Open-Air Exploration //

Breath of the Wild uses height and visibility to encourage exploration. From elevated locations, players can identify interesting landmarks and choose where to travel next.

Climbing allows many surfaces to become potential routes, reducing the importance of fixed pathways.

The paraglider further increases freedom by allowing players to travel from high locations across large distances.

Systemic Gameplay //

Objects and environmental elements follow systems that can interact with one another.

Fire can spread, metal can conduct electricity and objects can react to forces within the environment.

Because these systems interact, players can often discover solutions that were not presented as one fixed sequence of actions.

Player Freedom //

Many of the player's important abilities are introduced near the beginning of the game rather than being gradually unlocked across the entire adventure.

This allows challenges to focus on how abilities are combined rather than simply whether the player has unlocked the correct item.

The result is an open world built around experimentation, exploration and multiple possible solutions.

Why It Matters //

Breath of the Wild became an influential example of open-world design because it emphasised curiosity rather than filling the player's map with instructions.

Its climbing, physics and environmental systems gave players considerable freedom over both exploration and problem solving.
