GAME ARCHIVE // OPEN WORLD // 2011

The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim

The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim is an open-world role-playing game developed by Bethesda Game Studios and released in 2011. Players explore the province of Skyrim while developing their character, discovering locations and choosing which quests and activities they want to pursue.

Game Data //

Released: 2011

Developer: Bethesda Game Studios

Publisher: Bethesda Softworks

Original Platforms: PC, PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360

Genre: Open World / RPG

Archive // 002

Skyrim demonstrates how open-world design can be combined with role-playing systems and extensive optional content.

The player is given a central storyline, but much of the experience comes from deciding where to travel and which stories to follow.

Overview //

Skyrim contains cities, villages, mountains, forests, caves, ruins and dungeons spread across a large connected world.

After the game's opening, players are given considerable freedom to determine where they want to go next.

The main story can be followed immediately, delayed while the player explores, or combined with numerous optional questlines.

Exploration //

Exploration is encouraged by placing visible landmarks throughout the environment. A mountain, ruin or settlement seen in the distance can often be physically reached by the player.

Travelling between objectives regularly introduces new locations, characters and quests.

This means travelling through the world can create new objectives rather than simply acting as a journey between existing ones.

Player Choice //

Skyrim allows players to develop different skills depending on how they choose to play.

Combat can involve melee weapons, archery or magic, while other systems include crafting, trading and conversation.

Players can also join different organisations and follow independent questlines, creating different experiences within the same world.

Why It Matters //

Skyrim demonstrated the appeal of an open world containing enough optional content for players to spend large amounts of time away from the main storyline.

Its combination of exploration, role-playing and player choice helped make open-world design an increasingly important part of large-scale games during the 2010s.
